Six steps, the same for every change

Our software does these on its own. A person steps in only where you have said one should.

  1. 1

    It finds the work

    Spool watches for new package versions, infrastructure releases, changes in the APIs you depend on, security vulnerabilities and errors in production. Requests from your team land in the same queue.

  2. 2

    It plans the change

    Each ticket gets a plan before any code is written. Security vulnerabilities go to the front of the queue.

  3. 3

    It builds and tests

    The change is made in your repository and run against your tests. Tests that no longer match are rewritten and flagged.

  4. 4

    It checks against your limits

    You decide which kinds of change can go live on their own. Anything outside those limits waits for a Spool engineer or for your approval.

  5. 5

    It goes live

    The change is deployed to production through your own pipeline and cloud accounts.

  6. 6

    It is written down

    A changelog entry, a decision record and an audit trail are added to your repository, so anyone can see what changed and why.
